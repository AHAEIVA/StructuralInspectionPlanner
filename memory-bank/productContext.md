# Product Context

## Why This Project Exists:

Manual structural inspections are often dangerous, time-consuming, and expensive. They can also be inconsistent and may miss critical defects. Autonomous systems offer a way to perform these inspections more safely, efficiently, and thoroughly. This project aims to provide the planning capabilities necessary for such autonomous inspections.

## Problems It Solves:

*   **Safety:** Reduces the need for humans to work in hazardous environments (e.g., at height, in confined spaces).
*   **Cost:** Lowers the operational costs associated with manual inspections (labor, equipment rental, downtime).
*   **Efficiency:** Enables faster inspection cycles and more frequent monitoring of structural health.
*   **Data Quality:** Improves the consistency and comprehensiveness of inspection data through automated coverage and precise sensor positioning.
*   **Accessibility:** Allows inspection of hard-to-reach areas of large or complex structures.

## How It Should Work (User Experience Goals):

*   **Intuitive Workflow:** Users should be able to easily import 3D models, define inspection parameters, and generate inspection plans with minimal effort.
*   **Clear Visualization:** The system should provide clear visualizations of the 3D model, the planned inspection path, and sensor coverage.
*   **Customizable Parameters:** Users should have control over key parameters like sensor field of view, desired standoff distance, overlap between sensor footprints, and vehicle dynamics.
*   **Reliable Output:** The generated paths should be flyable, collision-free, and ensure complete coverage of the specified inspection areas.
*   **Interoperability:** The output should be compatible with common robotic platforms and mission control software.
*   **Feedback and Reporting:** The system should provide feedback on the planning process and generate reports summarizing the inspection plan (e.g., estimated time, path length, coverage).
